IThe position

“Employers and workforce agencies get returning citizens hired; S.I.P. keeps them employed, by clinically rebuilding the thinking patterns that cause job loss.”

Safer Foundation, Cara and Greencorps provide case management and readiness. CTA and Advocate Health provide jobs and training slots. S.I.P. provides the clinical thinking-change layer between the two. The differentiator is the combination: an LPC license, a documented curriculum with four measurable outcomes, and a model priced against what early turnover costs employers.

Pricing logic: direct-hire staffing fees often run 15 to 30 percent of first-year salary, so a retention program costing a small fraction of replacing an early quitter is an easy sell. Never charge job seekers for placement.

Outcomes to track from day one

Enrollment, completion, job placement, 30-, 60- and 90-day retention, self-awareness and reactivity scores before and after, and employer satisfaction. A one-page outcomes brief from the pilot is the single most useful sales document.

Warm targets

Advocate Health's Fair Chance Hiring Program, a CTA Second Chance partner agency, and one workforce delegate agency (Cara Collective or North Lawndale Employment Network).

VCompliance
  • Separate counseling from staffing. Illinois counseling ethics rules restrict dual relationships and referral fees. Have an attorney structure it.
  • Never charge candidates a placement fee. Legitimate placement fees are employer-paid.
  • Records. If S.I.P. handles participant records, HIPAA and 42 CFR Part 2 apply, alongside the Illinois Mental Health and Developmental Disabilities Confidentiality Act.
  • Fair Chance Ordinance. It limits when employers may ask about criminal history. It helps the pitch to employers and applies to S.I.P.'s own hiring.
  • Public copy. The public pages make no outcome claims, name no partner and say nothing about anyone's personal history. Keep it that way until the person concerned approves the exact words in writing.
