IThe gap

Chicago does not have a job-listing problem. It has a retention problem.

The transit authority, the hospitals, the city and a long list of employers will consider a person with a record. Safer Foundation, Cara and Greencorps get people ready.

Then comes the part no one is paid to work on. Workforce programs and employers describe the same pattern: people rarely lose a first job for lack of skill. They lose it to stress, to reactivity, to thinking patterns formed long before the job.

That is clinical territory. S.I.P. works there.

IIIThe method

Three practices, one discipline of thought.

Self-Improvement Philosophy rests on a simple claim: change how a person thinks, and the decisions follow. The cohort teaches that in three connected practices.

i.

Mindfulness

Noticing a trigger before it becomes a reaction. Participants practice the pause that protects a paycheck: the breath between a supervisor's tone and a reply.

ii.

Cognitive restructuring

Examining the story a person tells about a moment, and rewriting it with evidence. Self-awareness, vocabulary and critical thinking grow together.

iii.

Motivational interviewing

Participants name their own reasons for the job, the apprenticeship, the life they are building. Commitment that is spoken aloud outlasts commitment that is imposed.

VThe pledge

Three promises we will not bend.

Never a fee to the participant.

Employers, agencies and funders pay for the program. A job seeker is never charged for services, for placement or for a referral.

Always by choice.

Participation is voluntary and never a condition of hire or continued employment. Anyone may step away at any time without consequence.

Counseling stays private.

What is said in session stays in session. Partners receive aggregate, de-identified results, and attendance only with a participant's written, revocable consent.
